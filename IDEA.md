**INTRODUCTION**  
**Project Context**  
The construction industry in the Philippines, particularly within the heavy equipment rental sector, remains heavily reliant on traditional, paper-based administrative workflows. Almara Construction Company, an MSME located in Quezon City that specializes in leasing earth-moving and hauling equipment (including excavators, bulldozers, self-loading trucks, and dump trucks), reflects this broader industry trend in which managing a fleet of heavy machinery involves fragmented documentation and manual tracking. These legacy processes often lead to significant operational challenges, such as disorganized record-keeping, delayed billing, and a lack of real-time visibility into equipment utilization. As demand for infrastructure development in Metro Manila grows, the need for a more structured, digitized management approach becomes critical to maintaining a competitive edge and operational efficiency.  
One of the most pressing issues identified in this context is the vulnerability of physical documentation to environmental hazards and human error (Ayalp & Arslan, 2025). In regions like Luzon, where extreme weather events are frequent, and the rugged conditions of a construction site pose a constant threat, paper-based "Equipment Daily Time Reports" are prone to damage, leading to irreversible data loss and financial discrepancies (Han et. al., 2024). Furthermore, the absence of a centralized digital platform forces administrators to rely on manual calculations for complex variables, such as mobilization distances, fluctuating fuel prices, and maintenance costs, when generating client quotations (Annunen & Haapasalo, 2024). This manual intervention not only increases the likelihood of revenue leakage but also delays the response time to customer inquiries, hindering the company's ability to scale effectively (Lee et. al., 2023).  
By developing a Web-Based Construction Equipment Rental Management System, this study aims to bridge the gap between traditional practices and modern technological solutions. The proposed system will provide a centralized digital infrastructure that automates the quotation process and digitizes field data through Optical Character Recognition (OCR). Implementing a “weather-aware” digital framework enables stakeholders to proactively manage climate-related risks and optimize scheduling, thereby reducing overhead costs and enhancing service reliability. Beyond operational improvements, the system is expected to have a significant impact on various aspects of the business. For example, it can help minimize financial discrepancies and equipment data loss by safeguarding documentation from environmental hazards, while providing real-time equipment tracking that supports more accurate information and resource allocation.  
Furthermore, the streamlined administrative processes and faster client responses can improve customer satisfaction and increase competitiveness in a rapidly growing construction sector. Consequently, this project serves as a vital transition for Almara Construction Company, moving from high-risk manual labor toward a data-driven operational model that ensures long-term business continuity.

**Policies and Procedures.**  
The Almara Construction Company is a heavy equipment rental company offering two services: self-loading truck services and heavy equipment rental. The self-loading truck service helps customers pick up any heavily loaded materials or equipment from their chosen locations, providing drivers, helpers, and paperwork to transfer the loads. While the rental of heavy equipment offers heavy vehicles for a certain period to complete projects, at an hourly rate, with operators to manage, and mobilization and demobilization services. Their customers are usually agents from companies or primary delegates of companies; the former is usually short-term customer service, while the latter involves long-term engagements that require rigorous, safe maintenance of the machinery and a committed focus on cultivating a sustained, trusted relationship with the client.  

**Self-loading Trucks Service** 

1. The customer inquires about service prices, requesting the information needed via Messenger or phone, mainly through other customer referrals or Facebook advertisements.
2. The admin confirms the customer's identity by knowing the company the customer comes from (initial identification). Then provide a rough estimate of the fees based on the total distance (km) to travel, maintenance, gasoline, driver, helpers, paperwork, and other buffer fees.
3. If the customer agrees to the price and the exchange of vital information occurs, the company will process the service order for the customer, including paperwork for the travel pass, route planning, and assigning available drivers and helpers. The customer will pay the initial deposit, which will be paid in full upon completion of the service.
4. After the service finishes, the customer's estimated price is compared with the actual overall expenses to calculate the company's total net income.
5. The completed transaction is recorded in a log, providing details such as start location, drop-off location, start time, end time, drivers and helpers, other expenses, customer basic details, and the customer's paid fee.

**Heavy Equipment Rental Service** 

1. The customer inquires about the service fees, providing information needed such as project location and service rental equipment rates via Messenger or phone calls, mainly known by other customer referrals or Facebook advertisements.
2. The admin confirms the identity of the customer by knowing the company where the customer comes from (initial identification). Then, provide the current fee estimate based on the total distance (km) for mobilization and demobilization, the rental rate per hour of equipment based on diesel prices, maintenance, and operator.
3. If the customer agrees to the price, exchange of vital information occurs then the company will process the rental equipment ordered by the customer such as a final check of identification, project type, and project location to ensure the equipment is appropriate for the rental used, paperwork for travel pass, strategizing routes, assigning available drivers, helpers, and operators.
4. The customer will pay the initial deposit, a minimum of 100 hours of rate, the mobilization and demobilization fee, before the release of equipment occurs.
5. Upon the rental span of the customer, a specific physical folder will be created for their specific project sites to manage the contracts, weekly billings, daily time records of the rented equipment/s, and other essential information.
6. The daily time record of the rented equipment is monitored by the rental company trackers at the same time as the project management trackers to ensure accurate inputs of usage.
7. The agreed inputs will be sent by the tracker to the admin, who will deduct the hours of usage from their deposit to ensure proper billings and statement of accounts at the end of the contract.

**Data and Process.**  
The administrative workflow for heavy equipment rentals begins with informal customer inquiries via telecommunication channels or social media platforms. Upon receipt, the administrator follows a manual verification protocol to establish the client's corporate affiliation before calculating manual price estimates. These hand-calculated quotes synthesize multiple volatile variables, including mobilization and demobilization distances, hourly rental rates influenced by fluctuating fuel prices, maintenance overheads, and operator labor costs. Following confirmation of terms and data exchange, the client is required to pay an initial security deposit of at least 100 rental hours plus mobilization fees to authorize equipment release.  
To initiate subsequent operational processing, the administrator handles manual travel pass documentation, route optimization, and personnel allocation. To manage the project lifecycle, the administrator also initiates a physical documentation folder for contract and record maintenance. Post-deployment, equipment utilization is tracked via paper-based logs, where field personnel reconcile daily runtimes before transmitting data to the administrator.  
This centralized, manual intervention ultimately serves as an operational bottleneck, as the administrator must manually deduct reported hours from the deposit to generate statements of account. As a result, these critical transactional records are archived in physical file cabinets, leaving the company's data vulnerable to environmental risks such as flooding and heavy rainfall, which pose a significant threat of irreversible data loss and operational disruption.

**Problem and Solution Statement.**  
Heavy equipment and trucking rental companies frequently experience revenue leakage due to reliance on paper-based workflows and manual administrative tracking, a challenge clearly evident at Almara Construction Corporation. This reliance results in frequently disorganized and difficult-to-monitor work, along with their transactional data such as bookings, contracts, and billings. It is challenging to efficiently manage equipment or arrange for maintenance due to human error caused by cluttered paper records and manual data entry. Moreover, companies like Almara also find it difficult to remain profitable and competitive amid severe weather delays and unresolved equipment damage liability disputes.  
To resolve these issues, a centralized web-based construction rental platform will be developed. The system utilizes an OCR tool to convert the site-gathered, paper-based Equipment Daily Time Reports and corporate documentation into digital data. This platform also includes a quotation tool to manage rental prices, along with important components such as base rates, dynamic diesel prices, and maintenance fees. By integrating real-time weather data, the platform also enables proactive risk advisories, automated breakdown logging, and a climate compliance log feature to map environmental liability incidents on sites, all via a centralized dashboard. Therefore, implementing this technology-driven solution will drastically reduce revenue leakage, safeguard transactional data integrity, and significantly optimize administrative workflows.

**Purpose and Description**  
The primary purpose of this project is to modernize heavy equipment and trucking rental operations, specifically exemplifying Almara Construction Company, by minimizing the inefficiencies inherent in manual, paper-based administration. It seeks to establish a reliable, secure, and centralized digital environment that integrates equipment tracking, financial quoting, and logistical scheduling into a single platform. The ambition of the study is to empower the company with data-driven tools that mitigate the risks of human error and environmental disruptions, ensuring that every transaction, starting from the initial client quotation to the final billing, is accurate, transparent, and professionally managed.  
The Web-Based Construction Equipment Rental Management System is a specialized administrative platform designed to oversee the lifecycle of heavy machinery rentals. It serves as a digital hub that automates quotation generation based on dynamic diesel prices and mobilization distances, while also providing a "weather-aware" dashboard for dispatching proactive risk advisories and tracking compliance incident logs. By leveraging OCR tools to digitize daily time records and corporate documentation, the system converts physical field reports into actionable digital data, enabling precise billing, assisted account verification, and streamlined fleet maintenance. Essentially, the project provides a robust technological solution that simplifies complex logistics, protects vital business data, and enhances the overall service delivery for both the company and its clients.

**Specific Objectives**  
The following are the specific objectives of the study:

1. To design and develop an Optical Character Recognition (OCR) module that automatically scans handwritten paper EDTR entries to extract active machine operational hours and equipment breakdown statuses.
  1. To engineer a weather-informed heavy equipment fleet management platform with real-time meteorological data to deliver weather insights, log environmental liability incidents, track utilization-driven asset maintenance timers, and deliver post-project impact reports to analyze delays and damages incurred from overriding weather alerts.
  2. To establish a secure administrative verification dashboard featuring OCR-assisted KYC tools that extract business registration identifiers (such as SEC numbers and TINs) for streamlined manual validation.
  3. To incorporate a secure third-party payment gateway that supports online deposit transactions and provides automated real-time booking status updates.

**Scope and Limitations**  
This study focuses on the development and implementation of a Web-Based Construction Equipment Rental Management System tailored for Almara Construction Company in Quezon City, Metro Manila, yet highly applicable to similar rental providers. The system prioritizes a structured approach to generating quotations by utilizing dynamic pricing attributes aligned with the company’s operational standards. Specifically, the quotation logic calculates costs based on total distance (in kilometers) for mobilization and demobilization, plus an hourly rental rate. These rates are derived from localized inputs, including diesel prices, maintenance requirements, and operator labor costs to ensure financial accuracy.  
The system features a client booking portal for generating immediate, printable quotations and a centralized administrative dashboard for managing the heavy equipment fleet. A key innovation is the Weather-Aware Module, which integrates the Open-Meteo API to provide critical system alerts. These alerts are designed to dispatch proactive risk advisories to clients and automatically log environmental liability records during severe weather disruptions. The software’s overall performance will be validated against ISO/IEC 25010 standards to ensure it meets global software quality benchmarks.  
To maintain research feasibility, the study is subject to several functional limitations. While the system provides data-driven logistical alerts, it does not include machine-learning-based demand forecasting. The Optical Character Recognition (OCR) tool is specifically integrated to extract data from two primary sources: operational inputs from Equipment Daily Time Records (EDTRs), including active runtimes and breakdown statuses, and business registration identifiers from corporate documents for the KYC dashboard. This feature is dedicated to ensuring accurate record ingestion and data processing rather than facilitating automated decision-making. Furthermore, while the platform remains fundamentally a management and quotation tool, it incorporates an interface connection to a third-party online payment gateway as an optional module for customers, without natively processing or storing financial account records within its database persistence layer.  
Geographically, the weather-alerting features and logistical optimizations are restricted to the Luzon region. The project's scope strictly excludes the use of physical IoT hardware or GPS sensors, relying instead on manual inputs and third-party data. Finally, the system’s real-time functionality is inherently dependent on the uptime and data latency of external providers, specifically Open-Meteo for meteorological data and the targeted web registries scraped to retrieve current local diesel rates. By establishing these boundaries, the study remains focused on optimizing Almara Construction Company's administrative and logistical workflows through a centralized, web-based platform.

**Definition of Terms**

- **Equipment Daily Time Reports (EDTR):** The principal paper-based records utilized by onsite personnel to document the actual operating hours and idle periods of rented machinery.  
- **Ipso Facto:** Denotes an immediate contractual or system consequence that arises automatically as a direct result of a customer's actions regarding the company contract.  
- **Mobilization and Demobilization:** The logistical processes of transporting heavy equipment to a project site (mobilization) and returning it to the company yard (demobilization) following the rental period.  
- **Optical Character Recognition (OCR):** A technology that converts various types of documents, including scanned paper reports and images, into editable and searchable digital data.  
- **Open-Meteo API:** A third-party meteorological data service employed by the system to obtain real-time weather information and forecast data for Luzon.  
- **Revenue Leakage:** The unintentional loss of potential income resulting from inaccurate tracking of equipment hours, manual calculation errors, or unbilled maintenance and fuel expenses.  
- **Overhead Cost:** The indirect, ongoing operational expenses necessary to operate Almara Construction Company, which are not directly attributable to a specific equipment rental or mobilization distance.  
- **Weather-Aware Module:** A dedicated functional component of the system that processes meteorological data to generate alerts for project disruptions, equipment safety, and automated environmental liability logging.   
- **ISO/IEC 25010:** An international standard for evaluating the quality of software products based on characteristics such as functional suitability, performance efficiency, and security.  
- **JSON Web Token (JWT):** An open standard compact string utilized to securely transmit digitally signed, self-contained data between a client and a server, primarily for user authentication and authorization.

**Digitalization of Construction and Heavy Equipment Logistics**  
The construction industry is situated in a paradoxical manner: it is regarded as one of the least productive sectors worldwide, yet it has a substantial impact on the economy (Riazi et al., 2020; Segorina et al., 2022). The industry comprises a wide range of work involving the creation of buildings and infrastructure, from small residential units to large public facilities. Despite the looping processes of planning and financing, adhering to government regulations, managing suppliers, manufacturers, transporters, etc., construction techniques have become more intricate to achieve the goals of a project (Wilson, 2023).  
The shift from owning to renting equipment has been a global trend in the construction industry. According to Andy Agoos (2023), an industry veteran, renting equipment is now more advantageous than owning it due to shifts in the economic and technological landscapes. In the Philippine context, the construction rental market size is projected to grow at a compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of 5.06% by 2029 and 7.6% in 2032 (Arizton Advisory & Intelligence, 2024; Deep, 2024). This growth is primarily due to increased infrastructure investment through the Build Better More program of the current administration. While this surge in demand presents significant revenue opportunities for local equipment rental companies, it also exposes a crucial operational gap. Until now, most local rental companies have adopted a reactive approach to service management, leading to a mismatch between equipment availability and project requirements. This inefficiency creates an issue that transforms surging demand into project-wide missed opportunities.  
In addition, many SMEs rely on manual or experience-based decision-making when scheduling equipment or managing operations. However, research shows that traditional approaches are often inefficient and cannot handle complex variables such as demand, equipment condition, and scheduling constraints. Thus, it highlights the importance of data-driven equipment management systems to improve operational efficiency and decision-making in construction environments (Rachman & Firdaus, 2024).  
Most rental businesses suffer from delayed billing, especially in the construction industry, where projects often tally expenses at the project or monthly level, causing serious cash-flow issues on the rental business side (Takawira, 2025). To solve this without disrupting the natural flow of rental companies' processes for tallying hours of rent, micro-transactions enabled by digitalization are an effective way to create an audit trail of transactions and billings. This allows rental companies to seamlessly transition customers to their preferred billing format, proactively ensure daily deductions are accurate and correct with minimal supervision, and reduce Day Sales Outstanding (DSO) (Grüschow et al., 2016).  
Moreover, the transition from verbal agreements to immutable digital logs (blockchain) signals a fundamental shift in Philippine operational efficiency, effectively mitigating the trust issues that often create disputes between local labor and project management. By integrating digitalization into transactions, businesses can eliminate the friction of verifying actual equipment runtime versus idle time, providing a legitimate source of truth (Berenjestanaki, 2024). However, promoting advanced levels of digitalization alone in this type of industry is undeniably complex due to a significant digital literacy and devices gap within the local workforce, where many construction and rental personnel remain accustomed to a traditional, paper-based workforce (International Labour Organization, 2026).  
To ensure inclusivity, the system does not rely solely on a high-tech interface that could alienate less educated stakeholders. Instead, it utilizes digitalization to seamlessly back-end the existing paper-based processes, converting physical logs into digital entries through mobile scanning or simplified data-capture tools. This hybrid approach acts as a supportive bridge, allowing workers to maintain familiar routines while the system automatically generates the audit trail necessary for modern project accountability (Atlanta Industries Inc., 2026).  
Furthermore, the shift toward asset-light operations through digitalized “pay-as-you-use” (PAYG) models is a strategic financial evolution for modern contractors, particularly in the Philippines, where volatile climates prevail. By leveraging the Equipment-as-a-Service (EaaS) framework, powered by digital ledgers, contractors can transition from fixed to variable cost structures, ensuring that billing is strictly tied to machine activity rather than mere span possession (Evcenko et al., 2025). This digitalized deposit deduction system optimizes working capital management for both firms, allowing for the redirection of liquidity toward high-impact materials and labor for the project management side and sales for the rental business side.

**Optical Character Recognition (OCR) in Business Process Automation**  
Construction rental companies in the Philippines heavily rely on identification and document verification by simply looking and confirming the person transacting via call cards and phones. This traditional approach is rooted in an organizational culture that prioritizes informal coordination and social capital, where interpersonal familiarity historically served as the primary indicator of trust. However, recent research on the digital transformation of Philippine SMEs suggests that this look-and-confirm model is increasingly viewed as a bottleneck, as the post-pandemic business environment necessitates a shift toward digital competitiveness and formal technological safeguards (Vlačić et al., 2021; Verhoef et al., 2021). By migrating these transactions to digital platforms, businesses can move beyond inheritance-based trust, which relies on social circles, to a more resilient system of digital trust mediated by standardized identity verification (IDfy, 2026).  
Standardized Philippine identification cards (IDs), such as the PhilSys ID, Driver’s License, and UMID, utilize fixed layouts that facilitate high-precision data recovery through Zonal OCR or coordinate-based extraction. Rather than relying on a black-box AI that guesses text based on probabilistic patterns, a system that predefines the (x, y) coordinates of specific fields like "Name" and "ID Number" ensures a more deterministic result for these structured identity documents. Technical research supports this preference for structured mapping; studies demonstrate that while general-purpose OCR often achieves only moderate accuracy on business documents, the use of spatial Index Pairing can boost extraction reliability from approximately 60.18% to over 90.06% (de Jager & Nel, 2019). By leveraging the predictable layouts enforced by the Land Transportation Office (LTO) and the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA), rental platforms can achieve near-perfect extraction accuracy while maintaining a lightweight system that can detect obvious document anomalies (Verihubs, 2026).  
The system uses a matching system to create a reliable record of work by comparing two separate actors. Instead of relying on a single signature, the platform compares the rental company's record with the version submitted by the project manager. This method follows the integrated project delivery approach, in which parties maintain their own logs to ensure transparency and shared responsibility (Khorsand & Peracek, 2023). By reviewing both documents, the system can identify differences in reported hours, preventing lost revenue before any money is deducted. This verification step acts as a neutral judge, reducing trust issues by requiring agreement between the two sides.  
The technical side of this comparison follows the practice of double data entry, a standard way to prevent human error and ensure data accuracy (Hallberg & Auer, 2023). When the system reads the two separate logs using OCR, it looks for any errors between the recorded start times, end times, and total hours. If the two records match within a small allowed range, the system automatically subtracts the cost from the deposit balance. This process significantly reduces the office work that usually delays billing for several days (de Jager & Nel, 2019). By ensuring information is consistent across two independent sources, the platform creates a strong trail of evidence for both managers and workers.

**KYC and Automated Document Validation**  
The landscape of modern B2B transactions necessitates the rigorous validation of compliance and client-related documentation, a process that is increasingly optimized through the implementation of AI-driven Intelligent Document Processing (IDP). By leveraging advanced natural language processing and machine learning frameworks, IDP facilitates the automated extraction, classification, and validation of critical documents, effectively reducing operational processing times by more than 70% while mitigating the risks associated with human cognitive fatigue and manual entry errors (Pingili, 2025).  
Furthermore, contemporary "Agentic AI" IDP systems transcend the capabilities of traditional optical character recognition by employing dynamic reasoning over visual layouts and textual semantics to enforce sophisticated compliance protocols, thereby eliminating the constraints imposed by rigid, template-based architectures (Islam et al., 2026).  
In high-value asset leasing platforms, such as heavy equipment rental systems, implementing robust electronic Know Your Business (e-KYB) frameworks is critical to preventing corporate identity fraud. Modern onboarding protocols utilize Optical Character Recognition (OCR) and Intelligent Document Processing (IDP) to dynamically extract structural data from primary corporate identifiers, such as Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) registration numbers and corporate Tax Identification Numbers (TIN). This automated extraction layer minimizes initial onboarding friction by converting unstructured document images into clean data fields for immediate format validation (Jeong et al., 2025). By performing automated comparisons between user inputs and OCR outputs, the system successfully flags surface-level discrepancies before any manual review takes place.  
While automated extraction optimizes early onboarding stages, fully autonomous verification remains structurally unfeasible due to data silos and security walls embedded within official regulatory infrastructure. Crucially, public verification portals, such as the Bureau of Internal Revenue's Online Registration and Update System (ORUS), utilize strict CAPTCHA protocols to protect their networks from automated bot attacks (Nanglae & Bhattarakosol, 2024). Because there is no reliable, secure, or legally compliant mechanism to programmatically bypass these CAPTCHAs, automated backend scraping and continuous live API lookups are effectively blocked. This technical limitation necessitates a hybrid framework where human oversight compensates for the security barriers that prevent fully automated data validation.  
To overcome these automation blockers, the platform bridges the gap by presenting pre-aligned OCR data alongside targeted clipboard tools within a centralized administrative review panel. The human administrator manually executes the final external query on the SEC and BIR portals, solving the security CAPTCHAs to confirm the real-time active, suspended, or revoked status of the corporate applicant. Implementing a Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) approach ensures that human judgment is strategically combined with AI capabilities for responsible and efficient decision-making when handling complex compliance checks (Zeghmouli, 2024). To preserve operational fluidity during this manual step, the system can utilize a provisional booking state that temporarily locks the equipment calendar while withholding the final contract generation until human verification is complete.

**Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) and Oversight**  
The restriction of system access to authorized personnel remains a fundamental pillar of B2B security in the construction industry. By implementing dynamic permission management frameworks, such as Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) and Attribute-Based Access Control (ABAC), organizations can effectively segment user capabilities and safeguard sensitive logistical data across the supply chain (Li et al., 2024).  
The establishment of these granular access controls must be integrated into a comprehensive regulatory framework for data governance, proactively addressing the heightened privacy and security vulnerabilities inherent in modern cloud computing and big data environments (Sharma et al., 2025).

**Technical Implementation of Web Security**  
The technical enforcement of security boundaries in modern B2B platforms requires integrating sophisticated authentication frameworks and proactive network defense mechanisms. For robust identity verification and stateless token-based authentication, specifically JSON Web Tokens (JWTs), , are essential to maintain secure session integrity (Radware, 2026).  
Standardized protocols for JWT deployment mandate the utilization of short-lived access tokens complemented by rotating refresh tokens and strict algorithm allowlisting. Furthermore, mandatory transmission via HTTPS is strictly required to mitigate the risk of man-in-the-middle interceptions and unauthorized data exposure (Radware, 2026).  
At the infrastructure level, defense strategies must remain proactive to ensure business continuity. Contemporary cloud hosting environments provide integrated L3/L4/L7 Distributed Denial-of-Service (DDoS) mitigation alongside Web Application Firewalls (WAF) to effectively neutralize malicious botnets and sophisticated application-layer threats (Vercel, 2026).  
In addition, the protection of sensitive backend communication is achieved through rigorous network isolation. By utilizing Virtual Private Clouds (VPCs) and static egress IPs, organizations can establish a secure perimeter that ensures internal services remain entirely inaccessible from the public internet (Vercel, 2026).

**Synthesis of the Study**  
There is a clear need for construction rental companies to migrate from their traditional paper-based business operations to remain competitive in the industry. However, small-scale rental companies are likely to be left behind by larger companies due to scale differences and the technical nature of the transition to digitalization. Given the industry's increasing reliance on digital booking systems, various researchers have identified suitable applications and components to ensure local construction rental companies can transition smoothly. These solutions include Optical Character Recognition (OCR) approaches for secure customer-business onboarding, integrated fleet management frameworks to ensure appropriate cash flow, and weather API integrations to enhance decision-support environments.  
Although these existing digital solutions suggest general operational blueprints, a significant gap remains in addressing the unique financial vulnerabilities of Philippine Construction MSMEs, particularly regarding revenue leakage and strained liquidity caused by manual record-keeping. To address this gap, the proposed system captures usage data directly within digital Equipment Daily Time Record (EDTR) fields to ensure real-time billing accuracy, effectively eliminating the delays and errors associated with traditional paper-based reporting workflows. This centralized platform further acts as a foundational gateway for small-scale firms to generate their first wave of reporting intelligence, providing critical insights into fleet utilization and customer behavior that were previously inaccessible to them.

It is engineered using the Third Normal Form (3NF) and employs lookup and junction tables to create a highly normalized and auditable data model, which effectively eliminates redundancy, prevents the storage of derived aggregates, and ensures a streamlined, scalable backend. This comprehensive digital infrastructure maps critical operational domains, including user authentication, KYC verification, fleet inventory, and the entire rental lifecycle. The architectural framework, detailed in the Entity-Relationship Diagram (ERD), establishes logical connections between primary entities, specifically linking Project Sites to real-time Weather Alerts, and integrating Rental engagements with Equipment Assignments, billing Invoices, and digitized Equipment Daily Time Reports (EDTR). Crucially, to safeguard financial accuracy and operational integrity, the model utilizes a structured approach for time-variant data management through dedicated rate cards while incorporating an immutable audit log to ensure the complete traceability of all sensitive administrative actions.

The development of the Web-Based Construction Equipment Rental Management System is guided by the process in Figure 26 called Agile Software Development Model, an iterative framework that emphasizes adaptive planning and continuous stakeholder engagement through incremental delivery cycles (Sommerville 2016). In contrast to the rigid constraints of the traditional Waterfall method, the Agile approach provides the necessary flexibility to refine system requirements as the project evolves, which is essential for addressing the intricate operational nuances and domain-specific challenges inherent in Almara Construction Corporations's administrative workflows (Dingsoeyr et al., 2021).  
This development process is organized into five distinct iterative phases: Planning, Design, Development, Testing, and Review and Evaluation. By maintaining constant feedback loops between the research team and client stakeholders, each iteration ensures that the resulting digital infrastructure remains strictly aligned with the functional objectives of the study and the global software quality benchmarks established by the ISO/IEC 25010 standard.

**Planning Phase**  
The planning phase serves as the foundational stage of the development lifecycle, wherein the research team conducted structured stakeholder interviews with the owner and administrative personnel of Almara Construction Company. These inquiries were strategically designed to elicit both functional and non-functional system requirements by examining the company's existing workflows, inherent pain points, and operational constraints as documented in the Technical Background and Problem Analysis sections of this study.  
During this phase, the team performed a systematic requirements elicitation process, translating the qualitative insights gathered from the interviews into formal, actionable system specifications. These specifications were subsequently organized into a prioritized product backlog, wherein features were ranked according to their criticality in eliminating revenue leakage and digitizing legacy paper-based processes. The resulting *Requirements-Features Matrix (Table 5* and *Use Case Report (Table 6 onward)* serve as the direct outputs of this phase, establishing a traceable link between the identified operational problems and the proposed system functionalities.  
The product backlog was decomposed into a three-level hierarchy consistent with Agile planning conventions. Epics represented the highest level of organization, each corresponding to a primary system module: the (1) Dynamic Quotation Builder, the (2) OCR-Assisted KYC Verification module, the (3) Usage-Based Invoice Generation module, and the (4) OCR and Weather-Aware Fleet Management Dashboard. Each epic was further broken down into user stories, which articulate specific client and administrator pain points in the format of outcome-driven narratives. For instance, within the Fleet Management Dashboard epic, a representative user story reads: "As an administrator, I want to receive automated weather alerts for scheduled deployments so that I can proactively reschedule equipment mobilization and avoid site-hold losses." Each user story was then decomposed into discrete, time-boxed tasks assigned to individual team members within the sprint cycle, ensuring clear accountability and measurable progress at every level of the development hierarchy.  
Furthermore, the planning phase encompassed the definition of the project's scope boundaries, the selection of the technical software stack, and the establishment of the development sprint schedule, providing the research team with a structured roadmap for all subsequent implementation phases.  
**Design Phase**	  
Upon the successful finalization of the prioritized system requirements, the design phase served to translate approved specifications into rigorous architectural and interface blueprints. This critical stage of the development lifecycle operated across three concurrent design streams: User Interface Design, Database Schema Design, and System Architecture Design.  
**User Interface Design.** The research team developed interface wireframes and high-fidelity mockups in strict alignment with Almara Construction Company’s established corporate branding, incorporating the company’s color palette, typographic standards, and visual identity guidelines. This brand-aligned approach was strategically adopted to promote user familiarity and accelerate adoption among the company’s administrative personnel (Interaction Design Foundation, 2020). All interface layouts were engineered following the principles of user-centered design, ensuring that navigation flows, form structures, and dashboard components were optimized for the non-technical end-users identified in the Peopleware section of this study (Norman, 2013). Furthermore, the interface designs were validated against established usability heuristics prior to proceeding to development, ensuring that cognitive load was minimized and that critical functions—such as equipment booking, OCR document scanning, and weather alert monitoring—were accessible within a minimal number of user interactions.  
**Database Schema Design.** The relational database structure was engineered to support the system’s core data entities, including equipment records, client profiles, rental contracts, daily time records, billing invoices, and meteorological data logs. The schema was designed in accordance with the principles of database normalization up to the Third Normal Form (3NF) to eliminate data redundancy and preserve referential integrity across all transactional records (Ramakrishnan & Gehrke, 2021). The Data Dictionary was produced as a formal artifact of this sub-phase, defining all table attributes, data types, primary and foreign key relationships, and field-level constraints to serve as the authoritative reference for the development team throughout subsequent sprints.  
**System Architecture Design.** The overall system architecture was designed as a three-tier client-server model, comprising a presentation layer (React.js frontend deployed on Vercel’s Edge Network), an application logic layer (Node.js backend hosted within a secured Virtual Private Cloud), and a data persistence layer (an encrypted relational database instance). This architectural pattern was selected for its modularity, scalability, and its capacity to enforce a clean separation of concerns between the system’s interface, business logic, and data management responsibilities (Richards & Ford, 2020). The integration points for the Open-Meteo Weather API and the OCR text-extraction engine were formally mapped during this phase, with secure API endpoint contracts defined to govern all third-party data exchanges.

**Development Phase**  
The development phase constitutes the implementation stage wherein the approved designs are translated into a functional, deployable software system through iterative sprint cycles. Each sprint is planned to span one to two weeks, with sprint deliverables reviewed by the research team and validated against the acceptance criteria established in the product backlog before progression to subsequent sprints (Schwaber & Sutherland, 2020). Development proceeds module by module, following the epic hierarchy defined during the planning phase, with each sprint targeting a specific set of user stories and their associated tasks.  
Development proceeds in a modular, feature-driven sequence. The team begins with the foundational infrastructure, establishing the backend API framework, configuring the database instance, and deploying the hosting environments, before incrementally integrating higher-level features such as the quotation engine, OCR pipeline, and weather-aware alert module. This bottom-up construction strategy ensures that each system layer is stable and independently testable before dependencies are introduced (Forsgren et al., 2021).  
Throughout the development phase, the team adheres to the following best practices to ensure code quality, maintainability, and security.  
**(a) Feature Branch Workflow with Environment-Based Git Branching and CI/CD.** All source code is managed using a hybrid strategy that combines a feature branch workflow with an environment-based Git branching architecture, aligned with Continuous Integration and Continuous Deployment principles (Humble & Forsgren, 2023). The repository is organized into three persistent environment branches: development (dev), staging, and production (main), each corresponding to a live deployed environment. When a developer begins work on a user story or task, a dedicated feature branch is created from the dev branch. Upon completion, the feature branch is submitted for peer code review and, once approved, is merged back into dev. The dev branch is continuously deployed to the development environment at dev.domain.com, allowing the team to verify integrated behavior in a live context immediately after each merge. Stable, sprint-validated builds are subsequently promoted to the staging branch and deployed to staging.domain.com, where pre-release validation and user acceptance testing are conducted. Once a staging build is confirmed production-ready, it is merged into the main branch and deployed to the production environment at the public-facing domain. This structured promotion pipeline ensures that no untested code reaches production, and that features are validated incrementally across all three environments before release.  
**(b) Secure Coding Standards.** The development team implements security-by-design principles throughout, including the enforcement of input validation and sanitization on all form fields, parameterized database queries to prevent SQL injection vulnerabilities, and stateless JSON Web Token (JWT) authentication with short-lived access tokens and rotating refresh tokens transmitted exclusively over HTTPS (OWASP, 2021).  
**(c) Component-Based Architecture.** The frontend is developed using reusable React components, promoting modularity and reducing development redundancy. This approach also facilitates future system maintainability, as individual interface components can be updated or replaced without cascading effects on the broader system.  
**(d) API-Driven Development.** Backend services are exposed through well-defined RESTful API endpoints, ensuring a decoupled architecture where the frontend communicates with the server exclusively through standardized HTTP contracts. This design choice enhances system portability and simplifies future integration with third-party platforms.  
**(e) Code Review and Sprint Retrospectives.** At the conclusion of each sprint, the team conducts a sprint retrospective to examine completed deliverables, surface process inefficiencies, and establish concrete improvements for the next cycle. All feature branch merges into dev are subject to a mandatory peer code review process, ensuring that logic errors, security oversights, and deviations from coding standards are identified and resolved before integration.

**Testing Phase**  
The testing phase is implemented iteratively and in parallel with the development lifecycle, with formal campaigns executed at the conclusion of each sprint and upon the completion of specific features. This phase serves to operationalize the Test Methodology detailed in the subsequent sections of this study and is strictly governed by the quality objectives established within the Quality Plan. To ensure comprehensive system validation, testing activities are structured across four progressive levels of verification.  
**Unit Testing.** This level is conducted by individual developers upon the completion of each discrete function or module to verify that specific code components produce the expected outputs for defined inputs, including both valid and edge-case scenarios. This process is critical for isolating defects at the earliest possible stage, thereby minimizing the cost and complexity of remediation. Within the OCR processing pipeline, unit tests specifically validate extraction accuracy against a curated set of structured Equipment Daily Time Report samples to confirm that the minimum 90.06% accuracy threshold, as defined in the Quality Plan, is consistently achieved.  
**Integration Testing.** Performed following the successful completion of unit tests for related modules, integration testing verifies that individual components function correctly when combined. Particular focus is directed toward the data exchange interfaces between the OCR engine, the billing calculation module, the weather API integration, and the relational database. Integration test cases specifically target the double-entry verification workflow to confirm that the system accurately reconciles and flags discrepancies between tracker-submitted and administrator-submitted daily time records.  
**System Testing.** This stage evaluates the complete, integrated platform against the full set of functional and non-functional requirements documented in the Requirements-Features Matrix. Test scenarios simulate real-world usage conditions for each defined use case, including client registration with OCR-verified KYC documents, end-to-end equipment rental booking, quotation generation, weather-triggered scheduling alerts, and usage-based invoice computation. Non-functional system tests further assess performance under simulated concurrent user loads and validate system behavior under the constrained network conditions of 3 to 5 Mbps specified in the Network Infrastructure Requirements.  
**User Acceptance Testing (UAT).** UAT is conducted with a purposively sampled cohort of end-users drawn from Almara Construction Company’s administrative staff, project managers, and active rental clients. Participants execute predefined test scenarios within the staging environment to provide structured feedback, validating that system behavior aligns with real-world operational expectations and that the interface remains intuitive for non-technical users. Any defects or usability issues identified during this phase are logged and prioritized in the backlog for resolution prior to the official cutover to the production environment.

**Review and Evaluation**  
The review and evaluation phase serves as the definitive stage of the Agile lifecycle, where the finalized digital infrastructure is rigorously assessed against the project's primary objectives and the ISO/IEC 25010 international software quality standards. This phase operationalizes the structured Evaluation Plan established within this chapter and is executed through two simultaneous assessment streams: internal Sprint Retrospectives and the Formal System Evaluation.  
Sprint Retrospectives are implemented at the culmination of each iterative cycle to serve as an internal quality control mechanism. During these sessions, the research and development team systematically analyzes the completed deliverables, surfacing process inefficiencies or technical impediments encountered throughout the sprint to formulate concrete improvement strategies for the subsequent iteration. This reflective protocol ensures that the development velocity and architectural integrity of the system are continuously refined across the project timeline.  
Formal System Evaluation is initiated upon the deployment of the fully integrated administrative platform. The evaluation utilizes a mixed-methods research design, synthesizing quantitative survey data gathered through a four-point Likert Scale of Agreement with qualitative expert insights from IT specialists and domain professionals. The quantitative data is processed through descriptive statistics—specifically the calculation of the arithmetic mean—to generate a definitive quality score across the eight core ISO/IEC 25010 sub-characteristics: Functional Suitability, Performance Efficiency, Compatibility, Usability, Reliability, Security, Maintainability, and Portability.  
The evaluation process engages a purposively sampled cohort consisting of end-users, in accordance with the Participants and Sampling Technique protocols. A target threshold of ≥ 3.41, representing the "Agree" interpretative range, is established as the minimum metric for system compliance with the study's quality objectives. Any sub-characteristic that falls below this quantitative benchmark triggers an immediate corrective action as defined in the Quality Plan, with the resulting remediation efforts documented as part of the project's final operational report.

**Test Methodology/Procedures**  
The test methodology was utilized to ensure the system corresponds to the software industry standards and crucially to the needs of the Almara Construction Company. This involves different levels of testing including real-world cases and scenarios, test environment, and software quality evaluations to ensure the proper system implementations before deployment.

**Unit Testing**  
Unit testing is conducted independently by individual developers immediately upon the completion of each discrete function or module. This level of testing verifies that specific code components produce the expected outputs for defined sets of inputs, covering both standard valid execution paths and exceptional edge-case scenarios. By isolating software defects at the earliest possible stage of development, the research team minimizes the technical cost and complexity of later remediation efforts.  
Within the application's core data workflow, unit tests specifically validate text extraction accuracy within the OCR processing pipeline. These tests utilize a curated collection of structured Equipment Daily Time Report (EDTR) samples to confirm that the technical parser successfully maps individual characters and fields. This validation loop guarantees that the module consistently achieves the minimum 90.06% data extraction accuracy threshold required by the project's Quality Plan before advancing to broader system integration.

**Integration Testing**  
Integration testing is performed following the successful validation of unit tests for related modules to verify that separate software components interact correctly when combined. The primary testing focus is directed toward the data exchange interfaces that connect the core functionalities of the platform. This process validates the data pipelines running between the backend OCR text-extraction engine, the dynamic billing calculation module, the third-party weather API integration, and the PostgreSQL relational database persistence layer.  
Integration test cases are designed to target the platform's automated double-entry verification workflow. The tests simulate simultaneous data submissions to confirm that the system accurately reconciles and flags discrepancies between tracker-submitted logs and administrator-submitted daily time records. Validating this relational layer ensures that calculated hours are properly cross-referenced before any deposit deductions are permitted.  
**System Testing**  
System testing evaluates the entirely integrated platform against the comprehensive matrix of functional and non-functional requirements established in the initial design phases. Test scenarios simulate real-world usage environments for each defined use case across the application lifecycle. These complete, end-to-end runs validate core behaviors including client registration with OCR-verified KYC documents, equipment rental booking, dynamic quotation generation, real-time weather-triggered scheduling alerts, and usage-based invoice computations.  
Non-functional system tests evaluate the application's overall performance stability and response thresholds under simulated concurrent user loads. Furthermore, specific test cases assess network adaptation capabilities by restricting the test environment to the minimum broadband configuration of 3 to 5 Mbps. This testing ensures that the platform handles form submissions and heavy document uploads gracefully under the constrained internet speed conditions typical of active construction sites.

**User Acceptance Testing (UAT)**  
User Acceptance Testing (UAT) is conducted within a simulated staging environment with a purposively sampled cohort of actual stakeholders. This evaluation cohort includes Almara Construction Company administrators, field project managers, and active rental clients. Participants execute a series of predefined operational test scenarios to collect direct feedback on system behavior based on authentic day-to-day business interactions.  
The core objective of UAT is to validate that the platform aligns perfectly with local rental business rules and that the interface navigation remains intuitive for non-technical users. Any encountered system bugs, layout friction points, or processing delays are logged systematically. These items are prioritized in the product backlog for code remediation before the platform receives official clearance for production cutover.

**Test Case Design and Test Scenarios**  
Test case design and test scenarios are structured to prove functional compliance with identified business challenges and technical system requirements. Each test case defines a set of precise inputs, linear execution steps, and deterministic expected outputs to systematically evaluate both successful execution flows and alternative error conditions. These scenarios trace the complete lifecycle of heavy equipment rentals, starting from initial customer identification and document verification to final financial ledger settlement.  
Specialized test scenarios are engineered around the platform's failure tolerance protocols to validate how the system handles degraded input data, such as smudged, blurry, or low-resolution physical files. If the OCR engine encounters an unreadable EDTR document, the test case verifies that the system completely halts automated processing, flags a data readiness exception, and prompts the administrator to perform manual input confirmation. Additional scenarios test scheduling constraints, verifying that the interface immediately blocks double-bookings and displays a timeline adjustment prompt if a user attempts to lock in a piece of machinery on dates that are already reserved.

**Test Environment**  
The test environment functions as an isolated and controlled technical sandbox that mirrors the production architecture to ensure reliable system validation across distinct hosting tiers. The client-side presentation layer is deployed on Vercel's global Edge Network, allowing developers to test asset delivery optimizations for users distributed across the Luzon region. The application logic backend and the encrypted PostgreSQL relational database instance are hosted within a secured Virtual Private Cloud (VPC) with static egress IPs to isolate internal data communication pathways from public internet exploits.  
To replicate authentic field environments, the deployment promotion pipeline utilizes separate development and staging branches mapped to active domains. Network simulation software is introduced within these environments to throttle connection speeds to the baseline requirement of 3 to 5 Mbps broadband. Testing within these throttled parameters ensures that high-resolution document uploads execute within acceptable processing latencies and that background synchronization loops for meteorological data operate reliably despite low bandwidth.
